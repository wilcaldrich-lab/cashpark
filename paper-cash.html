What a bill is made of

  • Federal Reserve notes are 75% cotton and 25% linen, with tiny red and blue synthetic fibers spread through the paper.
  • A note weighs about 1 gram whatever its denomination, so there are about 454 notes to a pound.
  • It takes about 4,000 double folds, forward and back, to tear one.
  • Crane & Co., a Massachusetts company, has supplied the paper to the Bureau of Engraving and Printing since 1879.

Sources: U.S. Currency Education Program, Currency Facts; Bureau of Engraving and Printing, FAQs.

Who still pays with cash

  • About 1 in 7 consumer payments in the Fed’s 2026 Diary (survey taken October 2025). Cash was the third most used payment type, behind credit and debit cards.
  • 76% of consumers carried cash in 2025, averaging $69. 45% kept an average of $364 elsewhere for savings or emergencies.
  • Households earning under $25,000 a year, adults 55 and older, and rural residents (about nine cash payments a month vs. six for suburban and urban consumers) rely on cash more.

Source: Federal Reserve Financial Services, 2026 Diary of Consumer Payment Choice (May 11, 2026).
