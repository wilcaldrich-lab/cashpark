Unbanked America: who still lives on cash

Who does not have a bank account, who is underbanked, and why cash remains a primary payment method for some households.

Last checked Nov 25, 2026. Household-survey figures are from the FDIC’s 2023 survey, fielded in June 2023 and released in November 2024, unless noted.

The 2025 survey report is not out yet. The 2025 Household Survey was fielded, but as of Nov 25, 2026 the FDIC’s survey page still presents the 2023 results (page last updated November 14, 2024). The FDIC’s FAQ, last updated September 30, 2026, says a new report is released every two years in the fall of even-numbered years. cashscoreboard.com will update this page when the 2025 report is published. Until then, 2023 is the latest official picture.

How many households have no bank account

A household is unbanked if no one in it has a checking or savings account at a bank or credit union.

The unbanked rate changed little between 2021 (4.5 percent) and 2023 (4.2 percent). That difference was not statistically significant. Between 2011 — when the unbanked rate was at its highest level since the survey began in 2009 — and 2023, the rate fell by almost half. Figure ES.1 of the report shows 8.2 percent in 2011 and 4.2 percent in 2023.

An underbanked household has a bank account but also used at least one nonbank financial service in the past 12 months to meet transaction or credit needs, as the FDIC defines the term in the 2023 report. Fully banked households have an account and did not use those nonbank services.

Source: FDIC, 2023 National Survey of Unbanked and Underbanked Households (November 2024), executive summary and Chapter 7.

Why some households stay unbanked

“Don’t have enough money to meet minimum balance requirements” was the most cited reason for not having an account in 2023 (42.3 percent) and the most cited main reason (23.3 percent). “Don’t trust banks” was the second-most cited main reason (15.7 percent). One-third of unbanked households (33.4 percent) cited a reason related to fees or a minimum balance as the main reason — bank account fees that are too high or too unpredictable, or not enough money to meet a minimum balance.

The rate is not even across households. The unbanked rate among working-age households with a disability was 11.2 percent in 2023, three times the 3.7 percent rate among working-age households without a disability. Unbanked rates have long been higher for lower-income households and for Black and Hispanic households than for White households at the same income level, though the report notes that those rates have declined significantly since 2009.

Source: FDIC, 2023 National Survey of Unbanked and Underbanked Households (November 2024), executive summary.

Cash is still how some people pay

The Federal Reserve’s Diary of Consumer Payment Choice is a different survey, of consumers rather than of household account ownership, but it points in the same direction.

In the 2022 Diary, reported in the 2023 findings, consumers without a bank account made 66 percent of their payments with cash, up from 2019. Consumers with a bank account used cash for about 25 percent of payments in 2019 and 17 percent in 2022. That 66 percent is a 2022 figure. It is not a 2026 figure.

In the 2026 Diary (survey taken October 2025), about 1 in 7 consumer payments were made with cash. Households earning under $25,000 a year, adults 55 and older, and rural consumers relied on cash more than others. Rural consumers made about nine cash payments a month, compared with six for suburban and urban consumers. 76 percent of consumers carried cash, averaging $69, and 45 percent kept an average of $364 elsewhere for savings or emergencies.

Rules that let stores refuse cash hit these households first. For the legal picture, see cashscoreboard.com, Cash laws.

Sources: Federal Reserve Financial Services, 2023 Findings from the Diary of Consumer Payment Choice (2022 survey year); Federal Reserve Financial Services, 2026 Diary of Consumer Payment Choice (May 11, 2026).
