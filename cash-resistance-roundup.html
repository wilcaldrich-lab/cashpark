Cash-resistance roundup

A short, sourced list of what happened to cash this month: new laws and delays, cashless places, outages, and digital-dollar fights. Each item links to its source. No commentary that outruns the source.

Edition covers items checked through Nov 24, 2026. A new edition goes up each month; older ones will move to an archive.

Cash-acceptance laws

  • At least 10 states now require many in-person businesses to take cash

    Nov 17, 2026

    Pew Research Center counted Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Massachusetts, Maryland, Montana, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, and Rhode Island, plus at least 10 cities and counties. Massachusetts has had a version since 1978. The rules are not identical, so the headline is a starting point. See our tracker for each text.

    Source: Pew Research Center

  • Maryland's cash law took effect Oct 1

    Signed May 26, 2026; effective Oct 1, 2026

    Between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m., many Maryland merchants may not ban cash, require a card, or charge more for cash on in-person sales of essential goods (food, water and other drinks, hygiene and cleaning products, health products, fuel) from $5 up to $300. Restaurants that mostly serve on site, vending machines, and some specialty shops are excluded, and stores may refuse bills over $20 or offer a qualifying cash-to-card machine.

    Source: Maryland Laws of 2026, Chapter 714

  • Illinois signs a cash-acceptance law that starts in 2028

    Signed Jul 31, 2026; effective Jan 1, 2028

    The Retail Cash Payment Act covers staffed fuel stations, grocery stores, pharmacies, and restaurants for in-person sales under $500, and bars "cash not accepted" signs. Exceptions include self-service checkouts, 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., and temporary sales-system failures.

    Source: Governor's bill-action release · HB 4592 text

  • King County, Wash., delays its cash rule again, to July 1, 2027

    Council passed Jun 2, 2026

    Ordinance 20074 moved the start date for unincorporated King County from July 1, 2026 to July 1, 2027, saying more time was needed to educate retailers and develop enforcement. It had already been pushed once from July 1, 2025. Pew's table still shows the original date.

    Source: King County File 2026-0121

Cash use and cashless stores

  • The penny is gone, and cash rules still apply

    Mint ended penny production Nov 12, 2025

    New York City's consumer agency says businesses may round cash totals to the nearest nickel, but may not refuse pennies, charge more for cash, or fail to disclose a rounding policy, because the city's cash-acceptance law still applies.

    Source: NYC DCWP

  • Cash is still about 1 in 7 US payments

    Federal Reserve survey released May 11, 2026

    The 2026 Diary of Consumer Payment Choice found cash was still the third-most-used payment type, with 90% of consumers planning to keep using it. Adults 55 and older, households under $25,000, and rural residents relied on it more.

    Source: Federal Reserve Financial Services

Outages

  • A power disruption took down card payments at UK pubs and shops

    Jun 23, 2026

    Worldpay said a power-grid disruption caused intermittent transaction authorization issues for some clients. Contactless payments failed at a number of stores during an England World Cup game, some pubs said they were cash only, and videos showed long queues at cash machines. It was a UK event, but a useful reminder of what a cashless counter does when the processor is down. See our outage explainer.

    Source: The Guardian

Digital dollars, stablecoins, and instant payments

  • Congress bars a Fed-issued digital dollar through 2030

    Became law Jul 11, 2026

    Section 423 of the Federal Reserve Act, added by the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (Pub. L. 119-101), bars the Fed from issuing a CBDC, or anything substantially similar, until Dec 31, 2030. It defines a CBDC as a dollar-denominated digital asset that is a direct Fed liability and widely available to the public, and exempts an open, permissionless, private dollar currency that preserves the privacy of physical cash.

    Source: 12 U.S.C. § 423

  • South Carolina bars government use of a CBDC

    Signed May 19, 2026

    S.163 (Act 208) says a state or local governing authority may not accept or require payment in a CBDC or take part in a federal CBDC test. Its CBDC definition excludes a digital asset backed by legal tender or government treasuries and issued by a private entity, so private stablecoins are not treated as CBDCs. The act also covers digital-asset mining and self-custody.

    Source: SC S.163

  • FedNow keeps growing

    Federal Reserve data updated Oct 20, 2026

    FedNow settled 6,376,467 payments in the third quarter of 2026, up 27.6% from the second quarter, with an average of about 69,309 payments a day. FedNow is an instant-payments system, separate from both cash and a CBDC.

    Source: Federal Reserve Financial Services
